“Not charging” is often a 65 W brick on a 100 W machine, a dirty barrel jack, or a pack at end of life — not Windows.

<Solution difficulty={1} risk="low" time="10 min" title="Prove the charger">
<Step title="LED and rating">
Look at the brick LED. Confirm the wattage printed on the OEM charger matches the laptop (a 45 W USB-C charger will not feed a 140 W gaming chassis).
</Step>
<Figure src="/images/notebook/battery/charger.svg" alt="Charger brick with a status LED next to a notebook" caption="If the brick LED is dark, stop blaming the battery." />
<Step title="Wiggle test">
At the barrel or USB-C port, a flicker when you move the cable is a port or cable, not the cells.
</Step>
</Solution>

<Solution difficulty={2} risk="low" time="10 min" title="Windows battery report">
<Step title="Generate the report">
In an admin Command Prompt run `powercfg /batteryreport` and open the HTML in your user folder. Compare design capacity to full charge capacity.
</Step>
<Step title="Turn off battery saver myths">
Battery Saver does not block charging. A 10% “care” limit in OEM software can look like a fault — check Lenovo Vantage, MyASUS, Dell Power Manager, etc.
</Step>
</Solution>

<AdBetween />

<Solution difficulty={3} risk="medium" time="15 min" title="EC reset">
<Step title="Embedded controller">
Power off. Disconnect charger. Hold the power button 30–40 seconds. Reconnect and boot. Many “plugged in, not charging” bugs die here.
</Step>
</Solution>

<Solution difficulty={4} risk="high" time="40 min" title="Pack replacement">
<Warning>
If the bottom case is warped or the trackpad clicks unevenly, the pack may be swollen. Stop. Swollen lithium packs are a fire risk — do not puncture, do not charge, take them to proper disposal / a shop.
</Warning>
<Step title="Replace with the OEM part">
Match the FRU / model. After install, let it charge to 100% once. If a new pack still refuses, the charge circuit on the board is the next suspect — that is not a first-time solder job.
</Step>
<StopIf>
USB-C port loose in the chassis needs board-level repair. Do not flood it with glue.
</StopIf>
</Solution>

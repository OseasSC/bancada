Notebooks throttle then shut down when they cannot dump heat. Fix airflow before you buy a new machine.

<Solution difficulty={1} risk="low" time="10 min" title="Surface, vents, and a simple load check">
<Step title="Stop cooking the intakes">
Use a hard table, not a bed or sofa. Leave the rear and side vents open. A laptop stand that lifts the back helps more than a cheap “cooling pad” with dead fans.
</Step>
<Figure src="/images/notebook/overheating/vents.svg" alt="Notebook with highlighted intake vents" caption="Blocked intakes are the most common owner-caused overheat." />
</Solution>

<Solution difficulty={2} risk="low" time="15 min" title="Software load">
<Step title="See who is heating the CPU">
Open Task Manager. If a browser, indexer, or miner-like process sits at 80%+, end the task (your own process only) and see if temperatures drop.
</Step>
<Step title="Power plan">
Use Balanced. “Best performance” on a thin laptop is a shutdown timer.
</Step>
</Solution>

<Solution difficulty={3} risk="medium" time="20 min" title="Firmware and drivers">
<Step title="Vendor updates">
Install the OEM chipset, embedded controller, and GPU updates — they often change fan curves. Avoid random fan-control apps that fight the EC.
</Step>
</Solution>

<Solution difficulty={4} risk="high" time="60 min" title="Dust, fans, and paste" tools={["Phillips screwdriver", "Isopropyl alcohol 90%+"]}>
<Warning>
Bottom-case screws are easy. Heat-pipe screws and paste are not. If you have never seated a laptop heatsink, stop at dust-out with compressed air from the exhaust (short bursts).
</Warning>
<Step title="Blow dust out">
Power off, remove power. Short bursts of air through the exhaust. Do not spin the fan at insane RPM with the nozzle — hold the fan if you can see it.
</Step>
<Step title="Repaste">
Only if you accept warranty loss. Use a rice-grain of quality paste, even heatsink pressure, and the OEM screw order. Uneven pressure cracks mobile dies.
</Step>
<StopIf>
Shutdowns continue after a clean fan path: the thermal module or the board may be failing. That is a shop job.
</StopIf>
</Solution>

Artifacts that survive Safe Mode and BIOS are hardware. Driver work is only for artifacts that appear after Windows loads.

<Solution difficulty={1} risk="low" time="10 min" title="Where do the blocks appear?">
<Step title="BIOS and WinRE">
Reboot to firmware. If sparkles are already there, skip Windows forums. If they only appear in one game, try another API or a different title before you buy a card.
</Step>
<Figure src="/images/hardware/gpu/artifacts.svg" alt="Monitor showing colored artifact blocks" caption="Blocks and sparkles that ignore the application are classic VRAM or GPU failure." />
</Solution>

<Solution difficulty={2} risk="low" time="15 min" title="Temps and cables">
<Step title="Power plugs">
Each PCIe power connector on the card should be a dedicated PSU cable when the vendor says so. Daisy-chained cheap adapters brown-out a GPU under load.
</Step>
<Step title="Heat">
Dust the card fans. Thermal throttle plus artifacts often means the cooler is detached or caked — that is a high-risk teardown.
</Step>
</Solution>

<AdBetween />

<Solution difficulty={3} risk="medium" time="30 min" title="Driver isolation">
<Step title="Clean install">
Use the GPU vendor installer with a clean install checkbox, or DDU in Safe Mode if the driver will not stay loaded. If artifacts persist in Safe Mode, stop installing drivers.
</Step>
</Solution>

<Solution difficulty={4} risk="high" time="40 min" title="Swap the card" tools={["Phillips screwdriver"]}>
<Step title="Known-good GPU or iGPU">
Unplug the PSU. Swap in another card or use onboard video. Picture clean on onboard and dirty on the old card: replace the GPU. Dirty on everything: RAM, board, or PSU rails.
</Step>
<StopIf>
Do not bake a GPU in an oven. Do not “reflow” with a heat gun unless you are a trained rework tech — you will lift pads. Do not open the PSU.
</StopIf>
</Solution>

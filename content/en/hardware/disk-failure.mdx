Data is the valuable part. A technician images first when SMART looks ugly.

<Solution difficulty={1} risk="low" time="10 min" title="Is the drive even detected?">
<Step title="BIOS list">
Reboot to firmware and see if the SSD/HDD is named. Missing in BIOS is cable, power, or a dead controller — not `chkdsk`.
</Step>
<Figure src="/images/hardware/disk/ssd.svg" alt="SSD with a simple health indicator" caption="Clone while the OS still mounts. Waiting for a clicky disk is how family photos die." />
</Solution>

<Solution difficulty={2} risk="low" time="15 min" title="SMART">
<Step title="Read health">
CrystalDiskInfo or the vendor tool (Samsung Magician, WD Dashboard). Reallocated, pending, or CRC counts climbing mean replace, not “format to fix”.
</Step>
</Solution>

<AdBetween />

<Solution difficulty={3} risk="medium" time="60 min" title="Clone">
<Step title="Image to a larger or equal disk">
Use a reputable clone tool from a boot USB if Windows will not stay up. Verify files on the clone before you wipe the original.
</Step>
<Warning>
Do not use unknown “repair” utilities that promise to resurrect a clicking HDD. They often finish the death.
</Warning>
</Solution>

<Solution difficulty={4} risk="high" time="45 min" title="Cables, NVMe slot, adapter" tools={["Phillips screwdriver"]}>
<Step title="SATA">
Swap the data cable and the SATA power lead. Try another motherboard port. A drive that only dies on one cable was the cable.
</Step>
<Step title="NVMe">
Reseat the M.2 screw (too tight warps some drives). Try a second M.2 slot if the board has one. If a known-good NVMe also vanishes, the chipset lane or BIOS mode (RAID leftover) is next — shop territory if you are not comfortable in firmware.
</Step>
<StopIf>
Clicking, grinding, or a drive that disconnects when it warms up: clone what you can and stop using it as the original.
</StopIf>
</Solution>

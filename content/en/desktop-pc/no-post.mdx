POST is the firmware handshake. If you never see a logo, Windows is irrelevant. A technician strips the machine to the smallest configuration that can beep or display.

<Solution difficulty={1} risk="low" time="5 min" title="Power switch, outlet, and front-panel cable">
<Step title="PSU and wall">
Confirm the PSU rear switch is `I`, try another outlet, and another IEC cable. Cheap cables fail more often than people expect.
</Step>
<Step title="Front power button">
If the LED never lights, the front-panel POWER SW cable may be on the wrong pins. Check the motherboard silkscreen.
</Step>
</Solution>

<Solution difficulty={2} risk="medium" time="15 min" title="Listen and look">
<Step title="Beep / debug LEDs">
Count beeps or read Q-LEDs (CPU / DRAM / VGA / BOOT). DRAM LED stuck is almost always memory seating or a dead stick.
</Step>
<Figure src="/images/desktop-pc/no-post/ram-slots.svg" alt="Motherboard DIMM slots labeled A2 A1 B2 B1" caption="Most manuals want the first stick in A2, not the slot closest to the CPU." />
</Solution>

<AdBetween />

<Solution difficulty={3} risk="medium" time="25 min" title="Clear CMOS">
<Warning>
Use the jumper or the CMOS button with the PSU unplugged. Do not pry a coin cell with a metal screwdriver across the board.
</Warning>
<Step title="Reset firmware settings">
Unplug, jump CLR_CMOS for 10 seconds (or remove the battery for 5 minutes), restore, and boot. Overclocks and wrong XMP are a common “no POST” after a tweak.
</Step>
</Solution>

<Solution difficulty={4} risk="high" time="45 min" title="Minimal hardware boot" tools={["Phillips screwdriver"]}>
<Step title="Breadboard the PC">
Outside the case if needed: motherboard, CPU with cooler, one RAM stick in the primary slot, PSU, and onboard video. No RGB hubs, no extra USB devices, no storage.
</Step>
<Step title="Swap RAM then CPU power">
Try each stick and each slot. Confirm the CPU 8-pin (and 4-pin if present) is fully seated. Still dead: board, CPU, or PSU — stop and bench-test with known-good parts.
</Step>
<StopIf>
Do not short the PSU paperclip test unless you are trained — a failed PSU can damage the board. Do not open the PSU.
</StopIf>
</Solution>
